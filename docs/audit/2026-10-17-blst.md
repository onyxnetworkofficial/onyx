# Dependency review: `blst` 0.3.17 for the time-lock (ROADMAP C50, 2026-10-17)

Branch `tlock-ibe` from main `16c453d`. Why: drand/tlock's IBE needs BLS12-381 pairings, hash to
G1 and G2 scalar multiplication, and no Rust tlock crate was acceptable (RESEARCH.md "Time-lock
crates", ADR-0027 amendment 12). Review C12-R2 named the route: our own IBE on `blst`'s safe Rust
API. This review is mine (the implementer's); the fresh cryptography review of the IBE is THREATS
C50-R1. Nothing below claims more than the files read.

## Unsafe surface (read in the crate tarball, `~/.cargo/registry/src/*/blst-0.3.17`)

- `src/lib.rs` (2,392 lines): 92 lines with `unsafe`, each an FFI call into the C library or a
  `transmute` between `#[repr(transparent)]` wrappers and their C structs; `src/pippenger*.rs`: 29
  more (multi-scalar multiplication, not used by Onyx). `src/bindings.rs`: 215 `extern "C"`
  functions (bindgen output).
- C: 23 files in `blst/src`, 11,684 lines of `.c`/`.h`. Assembly: 59 files under `blst/build`
  (ELF, Mach-O, COFF, CHERI), used only on x86_64 and aarch64 (`build.rs`); every other target,
  wasm32 included, builds the portable C with `__BLST_NO_ASM__`.
- `build.rs` probes the build host's CPU for ADX on x86_64 (`is_x86_feature_detected!("adx")`)
  unless `target-cpu=` is set: a native binary built on an ADX machine needs ADX at run time
  (blst's README: the `portable` feature avoids it). Results do not depend on it.
- On native targets with `std` the crate starts a `threadpool` lazily for its multi-threaded
  calls (`miller_loop_n`, aggregate verification, MSM). Onyx calls none of them: `onyx-tlock`
  uses only `min_sig::SecretKey::{from_bytes, sk_to_pk, sign}`, `PublicKey`/`Signature`
  `uncompress`, `validate`, `compress`, the `From` conversions to `blst_p1_affine`/`blst_p2_affine`,
  `blst_fp12::miller_loop`, `final_exp`, `to_bendian` and `PartialEq`. It verifies round
  signatures with two pairings of its own instead of `Signature::verify` (which on `std` goes
  through `aggregate_verify` and the pool).
- Onyx side: `crates/onyx-tlock` holds no `unsafe` (`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, on top of the
  workspace's `unsafe_code = "deny"`); `scripts/check-deps.sh` fails on any `unsafe` block, fn,
  impl or `allow(unsafe_code)` in it, on a `blst` other than 0.3.17 in `Cargo.lock` or
  `fuzz/Cargo.lock`, and (`deny.toml` `[bans] deny ... wrappers`) on any crate but `onyx-tlock`
  depending on `blst` (checked once by adding `blst` to `onyx-coordinator`: "bans FAILED").

## Constant time (what blst gives; read in its C source)

- Scalar multiplication by the secret `r = H3(sigma, tau)`: `sk_to_pk` (`r·G2`) is
  `POINTonE2_sign` → `POINTonE2_mult_gls`, and `sign` (`r·H(id)`) is `POINTonE1_sign` →
  `POINTonE1_mult_glv`: windowed ladders from `ec_mult.h` ("constant-time subroutines"), followed
  by a Fermat inversion (`flt_reciprocal_fp`/`_fp2`) for the affine form.
- The final exponentiation inverts with `ct_inverse_mod_384` (`recip.c`).
- Not checked line by line: hash to G1 (SSWU) and the Miller loop; the Miller loop runs a fixed
  schedule over the curve's public parameter.
- Not constant time in Onyx's code: drand's H3 rejection loop (the number of SHA-256 rounds
  depends on `(sigma, tau)`; Go does the same and it shows only how many hash outputs were out of
  range; its range test on the accepted scalar has no early exit since review C50-R1-2); the
  comparisons in `open` touch public data only (ciphertext, a published signature).
  `SecretKey` zeroizes on drop; `sigma` and `tau` themselves are plain `[u8; 16]` copies.

## wasm32

`cargo build -p onyx-tlock --example wasm_kat --target wasm32-unknown-unknown` builds blst's C
with clang 21.1.8 (`cc` picks clang for wasm32; `build.rs` sets `-ffreestanding`,
`__BLST_NO_ASM__`, `SCRATCH_LIMIT` and no threads there). The module imports nothing.
`scripts/check-tlock-wasm.sh` runs it in node v22.23.2: every vector of `fixtures/tlock` decrypts
and re-encrypts byte for byte, as natively (`tests/interop.rs`). Debug build: 6.6 MB module, 0.67 s
for the whole run. The web verifier does not link it yet (ROADMAP C51).

## Verdict

Acceptable for `onyx-tlock` only, pinned exactly; a bump needs a new entry here. It is the same
pattern Onyx already ships for sealed reveals (`secp256k1` = libsecp256k1 in C behind
`secp256k1-sys`, built for wasm32 with clang by `scripts/build-web.sh`). The IBE on top is ours
and is NOT covered by blst's audit: its checks are the interop vectors (RESEARCH.md "Time-lock
interop vectors"), the adversarial tests (THREATS C50-*), the fuzz target `tlock_open`, and the
fresh cryptography review C50-R1 (THREATS, 2026-10-17: 0 High, 0 Medium).
